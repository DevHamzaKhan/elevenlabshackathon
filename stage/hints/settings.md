# Settings (com.android.settings, Samsung One UI)

- **Main list:** a big "Settings" title, a "Search" icon (magnifier) at the top right, the account row, then sections: "Connections", "Connected devices", "Modes and Routines", "Sounds and vibration", "Notifications", "Display", "Battery", "Lock screen", "Security and privacy", "Location", "Apps", "General management", "Accessibility", "Software update", "About phone". `swipe up` to see the lower ones.
- **Fastest way to anything:** tap "Search" → `type` the setting's name, such as "dark mode" or "brightness" → tap the matching result.
- **Where things are:** Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and Airplane mode are under "Connections". Dark mode and brightness are under "Display". Volume and ringtone are under "Sounds and vibration". Do not disturb is under "Modes and Routines" or "Notifications", depending on the version.
- **Switches:** a row's text is the setting's name, and its switch is often a separate node described "On" or "Off". Tap the switch, then read the screen to see it changed. Some rows open a page instead of switching.
- **Quick settings:** `key notifications` opens the shade, and a second `swipe down` shows the tiles ("Wi-Fi", "Bluetooth", "Flashlight", "Do not disturb"). Each tile's description holds its state, such as "Wi-Fi, On". Tap a tile to switch it.

## What goes wrong

- These change the real phone. Never turn off Wi-Fi, mobile data, Bluetooth (the earbuds use it), Airplane mode, accessibility services or notification access unless the user said so in plain words, and say what will happen first. Bobby can lose his connection.
- Names differ between One UI versions. If a section isn't where you expect, use "Search".
- A page that asks for a PIN, pattern or fingerprint: stop and tell the user. Never guess.
- `key back` goes up one level. `key home` leaves Settings.
